HCU Responsibilities

Interpret the single accelerator pedal as the driver propulsion request.
Coordinate ICE and rear electric propulsion.
Monitor battery and electric drivetrain status.
Manage regenerative braking requests.
Apply operating limits and safety interlocks.

Existing ECU Responsibilities

The original ICE ECU remains responsible for engine control functions.
Engine control is requested by the hybrid supervisory layer rather than completely replaced.
The EV inverter remains responsible for motor-side electrical control.
The HCU coordinates the two propulsion systems at the vehicle level.
Control Architecture Principle

The custom ESP32 controller acts as a supervisory Hybrid Control Unit. It does not directly replace the original ICE ECU or the motor inverter controller. Its primary role is to interpret vehicle-level demand, coordinate torque requests, monitor system states and enforce hybrid operating and safety logic.

HYBRID OPERATING STRATEGY

Operating Modes

The active mode depends on driver demand, vehicle state, battery condition and system protection limits.

MODE 01

EV Mode

Rear electric motor provides propulsion while the ICE remains inactive, when system conditions permit.

MODE 02

Hybrid Assist

ICE and rear electric motor operate together to support higher driver power demand.

MODE 03

ICE Mode

The front ICE drivetrain provides propulsion when electric operation is limited or not required.

MODE 04

Regenerative Braking

The rear electric motor operates as a generator during suitable deceleration conditions.

MODE 05

Protection / Limp

Electric propulsion can be limited or inhibited when a critical fault or unsafe condition is detected.

ENGINEERING & SAFETY NOTE

Regeneration Is Conditional

Regenerative braking is not intended to replace the vehicle's mechanical braking system. The ESP32 Hybrid ECU must limit or inhibit regenerative torque when battery charging capability, motor speed, tire grip, HV status or other system conditions do not permit safe energy recovery.
